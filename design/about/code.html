Two people, a roaster, and a fig tree.

Two years ago, we took over an old picture-framing shop on the corner of Chalk Lane and Weaver Street. The floors were dust-caked parquet, and the air smelled faintly of aged gum and cedar.

We named the café after the stubborn, century-old fig tree thriving in the back courtyard—its roots cracked right through the Victorian paving stones, completely unfazed by London winters.

We wanted a place that roasts real coffee without pretension, bakes honest bread from scratch, and lets people linger without checking the clock.

yard

The courtyard fig tree produces about six baskets of dark fruit every September. When it does, Mara turns them into our cardamom-fig galettes until the branches run bare.
